# Explaining a rule

The writer wants to understand a rule of grammar, punctuation, style or craft, often about a finding in their own text.

1. Say what the rule is in one or two plain sentences, without jargon. If you need a technical term, define it.
2. Say why it exists, meaning what it does for the reader.
3. Show it with a short, labelled example of your own (not the writer's text), right and wrong side by side if that helps.
4. Say when breaking it works, and what the break signals to the reader.
5. If the question came from a passage of theirs, apply the rule to it. Quote at most two sentences, and say whether their use is an error or a defensible choice.

Keep to one rule at a time. Offer to go deeper rather than writing an essay.
